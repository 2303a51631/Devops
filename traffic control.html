Simulation Flow

Three-phase methodology used to design, train, and evaluate the DRL traffic signal controller (Figure 1 from paper).

⬡ Phase 1 — Setup & Calibration

STEP 1

Field survey at critical intersections in City X to collect real-world traffic data.

STEP 2

Collect vehicle volume, composition (cars & motorcycles), movement patterns, signal timings.

STEP 3

Build and calibrate a digital twin using CityFlow / SUMO simulation platform.

⬡ Phase 2 — DRL Agent Training Loop

OBSERVE (State)

DQN agent reads queue lengths and vehicle waiting times per lane as state input.

ACTION

Neural network outputs a decision: select next green light phase (e.g., "Phase A → Lane N").

REWARD

Positive reward if waiting time decreases. Negative penalty if congestion worsens.

LEARN (Update)

Reward updates Q-network weights via backpropagation. Repeat millions of iterations.

⬡ Phase 3 — Performance Evaluation

TESTING

Trained DRL agent tested against Fixed-Time system under identical traffic conditions.

METRICS

Average delay time, max queue length, vehicle throughput measured and compared.

RESULT

DRL: 82.8s delay · 35.75 queue · 1,829 veh/hr vs Fixed: 115s · 55 queue · 1,550 veh/hr.

RL Core Components

STATE

Queue lengths per lane, total vehicle waiting times, current signal phase, time elapsed in phase.

ACTION

Select next green light phase from available signal phases. E.g., keep current phase or switch to next.

REWARD

Negative of total vehicle waiting time. Agent maximizes reward = minimizes cumulative delays.

Research Gap

Motivation and identified gap from the paper — why DRL was needed specifically for City X.

The Problem — City X

Severe traffic congestion at Kapuas Bridge (Jembatan Kapuas I) during morning and evening peak hours in Pontianak, West Kalimantan. Traditional fixed-time signal systems fail to adapt to dynamic traffic flows.

Fixed-Time Limitations

Fixed-time systems rely on static schedules from outdated surveys. They cannot adapt to urban growth or changing mobility patterns. They perform poorly during traffic variations.

Actuated Systems Fail Too

Actuated controllers optimize only locally without anticipating network-wide traffic flow, often shifting congestion to adjacent intersections rather than resolving it.

ATCS Limitations in Indonesia

Area Traffic Control Systems (Bandung, Semarang) use predefined timing plans, respond slowly to incidents, and struggle with Indonesia's heterogeneous motorcycle-heavy traffic.

DRL Research Gap

While DRL was proven in Yogyakarta (DDQN — Gadjah Mada University), no study had applied it to City X with its unique Kapuas Bridge congestion and motorcycle-dominant traffic.

Why DRL for City X

DRL integrates deep neural networks to handle high-dimensional states (traffic camera images, multi-lane queues), enabling real-time adaptive signal control at complex Indonesian intersections.

Why DQN over Q-Learning?

Classic Q-learning stores Q-values in a table — impractical for City X's complex intersection with many possible states ("curse of dimensionality"). DQN uses a neural network to approximate Q-values, making it scalable to large state spaces like multi-lane traffic conditions.

Scalability

The DRL framework trained on City X can be adapted for other Indonesian cities (Jakarta, Surabaya) by retraining with local traffic data. It is compatible with smart city platforms and can integrate with public transport and emergency management systems.

Environmental Benefits

Reduced vehicle waiting times lower fuel consumption and CO₂ emissions by up to 25% (Wei et al., 2019). Relevant given rising urban air pollution across Indonesian cities.

Challenges & Limitations

Data Availability

Real-time traffic data in City X may be limited. Complex sensor infrastructure (loop detectors, cameras) required for full implementation.

Motorcycle Traffic

Indonesia's two-wheeled vehicle dominance and irregular driver behaviors require tailored state representations with motorcycles as a separate feature class.

Sim-to-Real Gap

Simulation performance may differ in real-world deployment due to unforeseen factors. Continual model retraining needed as traffic patterns evolve.
